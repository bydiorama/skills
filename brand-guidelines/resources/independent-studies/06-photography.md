# Independent Study 6: Photography

> Analysis of photography guidelines across 180 brand guideline documents (36 Diorama, 144 external).
> Corpus: `/Research/per-document/diorama/` and `/Research/per-document/external/`

---

## 1. Prevalence

Photography is one of the most inconsistently covered brand elements in the corpus. While most documents *use* photography in application mockups, far fewer provide explicit photographic direction.

| Coverage level | Count | % | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dedicated photography section with rules** | ~52 | 29% | Named section with explicit style direction, categories, or do/don't guidance |
| **Partial / embedded direction** | ~35 | 21% | Photography style implied through application examples, moodboards, or brief references within other sections |
| **Photography mentioned but not detailed** | ~25 | 15% | Section exists in TOC or is noted as present but was not elaborated, or direction is purely visual (no written rules) |
| **No photography content** | ~62 | 36% | No photography guidelines, moodboards, or direction of any kind |

### Correlation with quality score

Documents with dedicated photography sections average a quality score of **4.0/5**, compared to **3.0/5** for those without any photography coverage. The strongest-scoring documents in the corpus (5/5) almost universally include photography direction: Cisco, IOC, F1, HSBC, AB-InBev, Strava, Virgin Media, Docusign, and Nike Empower all have substantial photography sections. This makes photography one of the clearest quality differentiators -- its presence signals a comprehensive, mature brand system.

### Diorama vs. External

| | Diorama (36) | External (134) |
|---|---|---|
| Dedicated section | 7 (19%) | 41 (31%) |
| Partial/embedded | 9 (25%) | 26 (19%) |
| None | 20 (56%) | 42 (31%) |

Diorama documents are less likely to include dedicated photography guidelines, though several newer projects (D008-Marina Dorcol, D016-CORWIN, D018-NORDIKA, D025-Corvus Atrium, D033-NOVEBA, D035-WEM) demonstrate increasingly detailed photography direction -- a clear maturation trajectory.

### Temporal trend

Pre-2010 documents (NASA, Bell System, Expo67, Ferrari, Dreher, Deutsche Bank) almost never address photography, consistent with the "graphic standards manual" tradition that focused on mark reproduction. The inflection arrives around 2015, and by 2020+ roughly 40% of new documents include dedicated photography sections.

## 2. Style Categories

Documents that include photography direction define their imagery using a consistent set of category types. The most common taxonomy organises photos by **subject** rather than by mood or technical treatment.

### Most common category names

| Category | Frequency | Typical brands |
|---|---|---|
| **Lifestyle / People** | ~38 docs | Bumble, WeWork, Oyster, Hometree, Cisco, Strava, CORWIN, Marina Dorcol |
| **Product** | ~22 docs | Tupperware, Peloton, Burger King, Sony VAIO, AB-InBev, SHARK |
| **Architectural / Environmental** | ~15 docs | Marina Dorcol, NORDIKA, DSTRCT Berlin, CORWIN, WeWork |
| **Portrait** | ~14 docs | IBM Garage, HCMA, Docusign, Bumble, Howden |
| **Editorial / Documentary** | ~10 docs | Adobe, Cisco, Strava, North Face, Arts Midwest |
| **Heritage / Historical** | ~5 docs | Marina Dorcol, Stella Artois, Dreher |

### Named category systems

The most sophisticated documents define three to six named categories with distinct art direction for each:

- **Adobe (E019)**: Three named styles -- *Atmospheric* (spaces/details), *Depersonalized* (people not primary focus), *Personal* (captured in a moment, never posed). This is one of the most elegantly structured photography taxonomies in the corpus.
- **Cisco (E027)**: Three categories -- *Lifestyle* (human, photojournalistic), *Environment* (sense of place, strong focal point), *Product* (tight crops, monochrome cast for later colour infusion).
- **CORWIN (D016)**: Four categories -- *Projects* (architectural), *Internal* (company culture), *Community* (events/candid), *Illustrative* (nature close-ups as metaphor).
- **Bumble (E061)**: Three categories -- *User Photography* (selfie-quality, phone-like), *Product Photography* (slightly higher production), *Lifestyle* (candid connection).
- **Tupperware (E125)**: Eight categories spanning *Product Closeup + Hand*, *Product + Ingredient*, *Product + People*, *Product + Text*, *E-Commerce*, *Multi-colored Products*, *Products with Prints*, and *Product Families*. The most granular product photography taxonomy in the corpus.
- **IOC (E070)**: Six categories -- *Action*, *Emotion*, *Heritage*, *Editorial*, *Digital application*, plus diversity representation requirements.
- **Havas (E086)**: Four principles -- *Human*, *Energetic*, *Communal*, *Connected*. Real interactions only; no B&W permitted; no aerial photography; global diversity required.

### Single-subject brands

Some brands define photography by a singular subject emphasis rather than multiple categories:
- **Christopher Doyle (E077)**: Photography IS the entire identity -- the most extreme case in the corpus where photographic output constitutes the brand itself rather than supporting a separate visual identity system.
- **British Museum (E098)**: Collection objects ARE the photography -- "the most important element of the design."
- **Peloton (E038)**: Extremely prescriptive product photography (specific bike settings, knob orientations, shooting angle restrictions).
- **KALW (E118)**: Subject verticals by editorial beat -- Current Events, Sports, History, Travel, Nature, Science & Technology.

## 3. Art Direction Rules

Photography art direction ranges from a single philosophical sentence to multi-page technical specifications. The data reveals four levels of specificity:

### Level 1: Mood/Feeling only (~15 docs)

A single adjective cluster with no technical guidance.
- **PROSIGHT (D023)**: "Neutral, natural character; grey tones should be present. Professional, reliable, human."
- **Corvus Atrium (D025)**: "Warm or neutral tones; people should appear happy and relaxed."
- **Hometree (E122)**: "Modern, relaxed, positive, warm."

### Level 2: Style + Subject direction (~18 docs)

Defines both the photographic mood and the subject matter, sometimes with colour guidance.
- **American Express (E004)**: "Aspirational but attainable; authentic; at least two people interacting; diverse; rich saturated color; uncluttered backgrounds."
- **Motorola (E012)**: "Sharp, modern space; young, spirited casting; on-set or on-location; imagery can interact with outline logo."
- **Oyster (E066)**: "Step away from stock photos. Showcase remote workers enjoying flexibility. Focus on people, objects, scenery rather than technology."

### Level 3: Detailed rules with prohibitions (~12 docs)

Includes explicit do/don't lists, colour palettes for photography, and composition rules.
- **CORWIN (D016)**: Defines a recommended earthy colour palette AND a list of forbidden colours (bright reds, neon greens, cyans, magentas). Prohibits stock-feel imagery, staged group portraits, oversaturated landscapes, and food photography.
- **NOVEBA (D033)**: Separates *stock photography* rules (warm lighting, authentic moments, no artificial feel) from *property photography* rules (correct verticals, no wide-angle distortion, no depressive weather, no oversaturated HDR).
- **NORDIKA (D018)**: Includes an explicit "Don'ts" page -- no low-quality photos, no high-contrast/overexposed images, no meaningless stock, no colour-inconsistent photos, no overly complicated compositions.

### Level 4: Technical specifications (~8 docs)

Precise production-level rules including camera settings, lighting rigs, post-production standards, or mathematical composition rules.
- **Peloton (E038)**: Specifies exact bike setup (seat/handlebar positions), knob orientations, minimum 5-foot distance from environment, shoot from right side only, explicit list of forbidden angles and equipment.
- **Sony VAIO (E026)**: "Depth of field is key: soften the look to counterpoint harder lines of the VAIO branding. Human involvement is subtle and implied, not the focus. Inside spreads must have warmth."
- **IBM Garage (E063)**: Specifies camera angle for portraits (slightly below eye-level to "celebrate and champion" subjects), names a recommended photographer (Ryan Garber).

### Lighting

Natural light is the overwhelming preference. Approximately 30 of the 48 docs with photography sections explicitly call for natural or ambient lighting. Only a handful specify studio lighting:
- **Tupperware (E125)**: "Soft diffused lighting" for product closeups, "balanced/soft key lighting" for people shots.
- **IBM Garage (E063)**: "Bright, depth of field, warm."
- **AB-InBev (E075)**: "Warm red ambient glow" -- their distinctive "Red Thread" lighting effect.

### Composition

Composition rules are rare. Only about 8 documents specify composition:
- **Tupperware**: Rule of thirds, negative space for product shots.
- **KALW (E118)**: At least one curved corner on every photo (max three), matching the brand's rounded-corner motif.
- **Howden (E067)**: "Multiple subjects/areas of interest; avoid single isolated subjects."
- **WeWork (E119)**: "Symmetrical composition preferred to show architectural intention."
- **ASICS (E147)**: "Unexpected cropping" as a deliberate brand device.

## 4. Color Treatments

Color treatments for photography -- duotone, desaturation, filters, overlays -- appear in approximately **20 documents (12%)**.

### Duotone / Tint treatments

| Brand | Treatment | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **SHARK (D002)** | Brand-colour duplex | Full duotone using the brand palette; applied to social media imagery |
| **DSTRCT Berlin (D007)** | Violet-tinted interiors | Duotone overlay on select editorial spreads |
| **WEM Wingman (D024)** | Duotone red | Applied to select application photography (hand holding phone) |
| **Marina Dorcol (D008)** | Duotone for digital ads | Specified for display advertising formats specifically |
| **Research Ireland (E141)** | Gradient overlays | Brand palette gradients applied to third-party images for consistency and to mask quality issues |
| **KOYOK (E079)** | "Saudade" duotone | Duotone treatment in brand colors evoking the Brazilian concept of saudade -- nostalgic longing |
| **Wave Essence (E089)** | B&W + colored wave overlay | Photography rendered in black and white with a colored sinusoidal wave overlay in brand palette |

### Desaturation

- **DSTRCT Berlin (D007)**: "Warm desaturated/slightly muted color grade" for cityscape photography.
- **WEM Wingman (D024)**: "Warm, muted, nearly monochrome toning -- beige/sepia aesthetic."
- **TECHBOX (D010)**: Full black-and-white editorial photography, allowing the brand palette to own all chromatic expression in the UI.

### Warm colour grading

The most common treatment is warm colour grading rather than an explicit filter. Approximately 12 documents call for warm tones:
- **Marina Dorcol (D008)**: Golden-hour lighting, warm colour temperature.
- **Hometree (E122)**: Always natural light for warmth and optimism.
- **Corvus Atrium (D025)**: Warm or neutral tones required; cool colour schemes explicitly forbidden.
- **Zapier (E126)**: "Warm tones with peachy/amber cast."

### Photography colour palettes

Three documents in the corpus define an explicit colour palette specifically for photography (as distinct from the brand colour palette):
- **WEM (D035)**: Recommended colour palette AND an "avoid" palette specifically for photographic content -- ensuring tonal consistency.
- **CORWIN (D016)**: Earthy tone palette for photos (blacks, deep greens, grays, muted browns) with a forbidden list (bright reds, neon greens, cyans).
- **AB-InBev (E075)**: "Red Thread" warm ambient glow as a unifying photographic tone.

## 5. Do/Don't Examples

Approximately **25 documents (15%)** include explicit photography "don'ts." This is relatively low compared to logo don'ts (which appear in ~60% of documents with logo sections).

### Most common prohibitions

| Prohibition | Frequency | Example brands |
|---|---|---|
| **No stock-feel / generic imagery** | ~15 docs | CORWIN, NORDIKA, NOVEBA, Oyster, Strava, Nike Empower |
| **No posed / staged shots** | ~12 docs | American Express, NOVEBA, WeWork, Strava, Bumble, HCMA, Research Ireland |
| **No busy / cluttered backgrounds** | ~8 docs | American Express, Howden, Tupperware |
| **No oversaturated / heavy HDR** | ~6 docs | NOVEBA, NORDIKA, Bumble |
| **No low resolution / poor quality** | ~6 docs | NORDIKA, Corvus Atrium, Crafts Council |
| **No ostentatious / luxury imagery** | ~4 docs | American Express ("no silver service, butlers"), Corvus Atrium ("no superlatives") |
| **No artificial filters** | ~4 docs | American Express, Bumble |
| **No camera-aware subjects** | ~4 docs | Howden, Research Ireland, NOVEBA |
| **No wide-angle distortion** | ~3 docs | NOVEBA (property photography specifically) |
| **No food photography** | ~1 doc | CORWIN (unusual prohibition for a real estate brand) |
| **No B&W photography** | ~2 docs | Havas (real interactions only, no black and white permitted) |
| **No aerial photography** | ~1 doc | Havas (unusual blanket prohibition on aerial/drone shots) |

### Brands with the most comprehensive don'ts

1. **NOVEBA (D033)**: Separate don't lists for stock photography AND property photography. Stock: no artificial feel, no staged poses, no cold/sterile lighting. Property: no extreme wide-angle distortion, no depressive weather, no bird's-eye/frog perspective, no noise/blur/oversaturated HDR.
2. **CORWIN (D016)**: No stock-feel imagery, no staged group portraits, no oversaturated landscapes, no food photography, no images with clashing non-brand palettes.
3. **NORDIKA (D018)**: No low-quality photos, no high-contrast/overexposed images, no meaningless stock, no colour-inconsistent photos, no overly complicated compositions.
4. **American Express (E004)**: No busy backgrounds, no stereotypical images, no ostentatious subject matter, no artificial filters, no posed shots.
5. **Bumble (E061)**: No heavy retouching, no heavy contrast, no overuse of depth of field, no heavy patterns/prints or visible clothing branding in styling.

## 6. Stock vs. Custom

Only **~12 documents (7%)** explicitly address the stock-vs-custom photography question. This is a significant gap given that most brands rely heavily on stock imagery.

### Anti-stock stance

- **Oyster (E066)**: "Step away from stock photos" -- explicitly stated as strategic direction.
- **Nike Empower (E146)**: "Not Fake" -- visceral rejection of anything staged or stock-feeling.
- **Strava (E090)**: Athletes shown "as they are: imperfectly beautiful" -- no stock imagery could achieve this.
- **CORWIN (D016)**: "No stock-feel generic imagery."
- **NOVEBA (D033)**: Dedicated section forbidding "generic stock photos with artificial feel."

### Stock guidance with guardrails

- **Adobe (E019)**: Stock photography must be from **Adobe Stock** specifically and requires brand review before licensing. This is the most specific stock-sourcing rule in the corpus.
- **NorthAlley (E145)**: Recommends specific free stock sources -- Unsplash, Freepik, Pixabay, Pexels.
- **Crafts Council (E137)**: Resolution minimums (72dpi on-screen, 300dpi in print) serve as a quality gate for any sourced imagery.

### Custom photography emphasis

- **Cisco (E027)**: Maintains its own extensive image library.
- **RAC (E015)**: Operates an internal "RAC Media Garage" for brand asset creation and curation.
- **Barbican (E022)**: Image libraries maintained per art form, centrally managed via printdesign@barbican.org.uk.
- **IOC (E070)**: Photography categories imply bespoke shoots (Action, Emotion, Heritage) rather than stock sourcing.

### AI-generated imagery

One document pioneers AI imagery as an official brand methodology:
- **Wave (E064)**: Uses **Midjourney-generated imagery** with specific prompts for wave images (glass sculpture compositions) and face/portrait images. The prompts are included as official brand assets. This is the only document in the 180-document corpus to formalise AI image generation.

## 7. Model/Casting & Diversity

Approximately **15 documents (9%)** address casting, diversity, or representation in photography. This is a growing area concentrated almost entirely in post-2018 documents.

### Explicit diversity guidelines

| Brand | Diversity requirements |
|---|---|
| **American Express (E004)** | "Diversity in gender, ethnicity, age, body types" -- one of the earliest diversity-in-photography requirements observed |
| **TikTok (E016)** | Diversity explicitly called out as a photography section -- "progressive and intentional" |
| **Bumble (E061)** | "Diverse range of gender, race, age, sexual orientation, body type, ability." Styling: "authentic to character; no heavy patterns, prints, or visible clothing branding." |
| **Brickline (E062)** | "Age, gender, race, ability, activity, geography, seasons, time of day." Community-sourced imagery encouraged. |
| **IOC (E070)** | "Explicit requirement for diverse representation" across all six photography categories. |
| **Research Ireland (E141)** | "Mix of ages, ethnicities, genders representing Ireland and the broader research community." |
| **SPACE10 (E139)** | "Diverse and accessible: inclusivity in visual representation." Warns to "avoid stereotypes; ensure respect and humility." |
| **Havas (E086)** | Global diversity required across all photography. Real interactions only -- no staged shots. No B&W photography; no aerial photography. |
| **Zip (E116)** | Images show racial and gender diversity -- documented through application examples rather than written rules. |

### Casting direction

Only a handful of documents go beyond diversity checklists to provide actual casting direction:
- **Bumble (E061)**: Styling must be "authentic to character" -- no imposing a brand aesthetic on models.
- **Motorola (E012)**: "Young, spirited casting direction."
- **Docusign (E074)**: Dedicated "Casting guidelines and wardrobe/accessories direction" section.
- **Nike Empower (E146)**: Photography defined by "a tapestry of creators, each bringing their unique point of view" -- diversity is structural, not cosmetic.

### Notably absent

Diversity guidelines are almost entirely absent from:
- All pre-2015 documents
- All Diorama documents (none include formal diversity requirements, though some show diverse subjects in mockups)
- Industry sectors: automotive, government, manufacturing, food/beverage (with the exception of AB-InBev)

## 8. Image + Brand Overlay

Rules for combining photography with brand elements (logos, text, gradients) appear in approximately **30 documents (18%)**. These rules range from simple logo-on-photo placement to sophisticated overlay systems.

### Logo placement on photos

The most common rule is simple contrast guidance:
- **Acura (E110)**: "Must maintain sufficient contrast for legibility."
- **McDelivery (E114)**: Detailed rules for contrast and legibility on photographic backgrounds.
- **Burger King (E135)**: Uses Primary Logo with Background (Mayo Egg White backing) to retain legibility on food photography.
- **British Museum (E098)**: "Text and logo must NEVER be placed over objects -- always respect and protect objects." The strictest anti-overlay rule in the corpus.
- **Tokyo Olympics (E014)**: Emblem placement over photographic backgrounds requires the boxed version.

### Text overlay systems

- **DSTRCT Berlin (D007)**: Bold colour blocks and typographic overlays integrated with photography rather than standing alone.
- **Generali Balans (D004)**: Horizontal red bars with logotype and social media icons overlay the bottom of images; large typographic quotes in white over imagery.
- **FONA (D003)**: Presentation slides specify black overlay at 40% transparency over photography.
- **Howden (E067)**: Multiple cover types including "lens" and "gradient" treatments that combine photography with brand elements.

### Gradient/Fade techniques

- **Research Ireland (E141)**: Gradient overlays using the brand palette applied to third-party images for consistency and to mask quality issues. Any palette combination valid.
- **Eterno Cloud (D028)**: Green gradient at top blending into photography below; logo in top corner.
- **Howden (E067)**: "Lens" effect and "gradient" effect as two of five brochure cover types.
- **DSTRCT Berlin (D007)**: Violet-tinted colour overlay on interior photography spreads.

### Photography as mask/container

- **Detroit Institute of Arts (E023)**: Images may be masked or full-framed. Masked images can replace one letter of the wordmark (image should have similar visual structure to the replaced letter).
- **Arthaus (E021)**: Photography visible through letterforms (logo-as-mask technique) with organic/fluid imagery.
- **REMPO (D009)**: Photography fills the rounded border (oramovanie) framing device.
- **KALW (E118)**: Photos always have at least one curved corner, matching brand's rounded-corner motif.

## 9. Best Examples -- Top 5 Photography Sections

### 1. Bumble (E061) -- Best overall photography direction

Five photography principles (Inclusiveness, Authenticity, Connection, Empowerment, Fun). Three clearly defined categories (User, Product, Lifestyle) with distinct production values for each. Detailed casting and styling guidelines including diversity across "gender, race, age, sexual orientation, body type, ability." Technical direction covering white balance, retouching limits, depth-of-field guidance, and brand colour integration through wardrobe/location. The deliberate instruction that user photos should look like "high-quality phone photos" is a rare and honest production calibration.

### 2. Tupperware (E125) -- Best product photography taxonomy

The most granular photography taxonomy in the corpus with 8+ named categories, each with specific lighting, composition, and background rules. "Our products look good on a shelf. But they're truly beautiful in action" sets a clear philosophical direction. Category-specific rules for backgrounds (tint from Color Library, match to product colour family, Tupperware Blue as default) demonstrate how a photography system can integrate with a colour system.

### 3. CORWIN (D016) -- Best real-estate photography direction

Four named categories (Projects, Internal, Community, Illustrative) with a recommended earthy colour palette AND an explicit list of forbidden colours for photography. The "Illustrative" category (nature close-ups as sustainability metaphor) is an unusually thoughtful addition. Clear don'ts: no stock-feel, no staged portraits, no oversaturated landscapes, no food photography.

### 4. Cisco (E027) -- Best corporate/enterprise photography

Three categories (Lifestyle, Environment, Product) with strong philosophical grounding: "Real people in real situations in real environments. Never contrived, trite, or cliched." The product photography direction to shoot with a "predominant monochrome cast for later colour/graphic infusion" is a practical production insight rarely seen in brand guidelines. Consistency across still photography, video, and motion graphics explicitly required.

### 5. Nike Empower (E146) -- Best campaign photography philosophy

"Visceral. Real. Unapologetic. Disarming. Alive. Not Fake." No single art direction or colour filter -- visual identity defined by "a tapestry of creators, each bringing their unique point of view." Direction to celebrate the full range of athlete emotions and behind-the-scenes moments. The anti-photography-direction direction -- trusting diverse creators within guardrails -- is a radical and effective approach at 270 pages of campaign guidelines.

### Honourable mentions

- **NOVEBA (D033)**: Dual photography ruleset (stock vs. property) with separate do/don't lists for each -- the most industry-specific approach.
- **Adobe (E019)**: Elegant three-category taxonomy (Atmospheric, Depersonalized, Personal) with stock-sourcing rules (Adobe Stock only).
- **AB-InBev (E075)**: The "Red Thread" warm ambient glow as a unifying photographic treatment is the most distinctive single photographic device in the corpus.
- **IBM Garage (E063)**: Two styles (People/Studio, Places/Location) with specific camera angle for portraits (below eye-level to "celebrate and champion") and a named photographer recommendation.

## 10. Toolkit Implications -- Photography Direction Template

Based on the patterns observed across 180 documents, a photography direction template for the Service Portal should include:

### Recommended template structure

```
1. Photography Philosophy (1 paragraph)
   - What role does photography play in the brand?
   - What feeling should images create?

2. Style Categories (2-6 named categories)
   - Category name
   - Subject description
   - Lighting direction
   - Composition notes
   - Example reference (moodboard slot)

3. Colour Direction
   - Recommended colour temperature (warm/neutral/cool)
   - Palette-aligned colour guidance (optional: explicit photo colour palette)
   - Forbidden colours / tones

4. Casting & Representation
   - Diversity requirements
   - Styling direction (wardrobe, props, setting)
   - Mood/expression guidance

5. Technical Standards
   - Lighting preference (natural/studio/mixed)
   - Retouching limits
   - Minimum resolution
   - Aspect ratios for key touchpoints

6. Do's and Don'ts
   - 5-8 do's with example references
   - 5-8 don'ts with example references

7. Stock Photography Rules
   - Approved sources (if applicable)
   - Quality/style gates for stock selection
   - Review/approval process

8. Brand Integration
   - Logo-on-photo rules (contrast, exclusion zones)
   - Text overlay guidelines
   - Colour treatment rules (duotone, gradient, filter)
   - Photography-as-container rules (masking, cropping into brand shapes)
```

### Key design decisions for the template

1. **Category count**: Most effective systems use 3-4 categories. Fewer than 3 lacks nuance; more than 6 becomes unwieldy. The sweet spot observed in the best-scoring documents is **3 named categories** (e.g., Adobe's Atmospheric/Depersonalized/Personal; Cisco's Lifestyle/Environment/Product).

2. **Do/Don't format**: Photography don'ts are dramatically underused (only 15% of docs include them) despite being the single most effective guardrail against off-brand imagery. The template should make don'ts a required section, not optional.

3. **Stock guidance is critical**: Only 7% of documents address stock photography, yet the majority of brands rely on it. The template should include a mandatory stock-sourcing section with quality gates.

4. **Diversity as default**: Post-2020 documents increasingly treat diversity as non-negotiable. The template should include diversity and representation as a standard section, not an optional add-on.

5. **Photo colour palette**: The WEM and CORWIN approach of defining a separate colour palette for photography (distinct from the brand colour palette) is a powerful tool that prevents the common failure mode of on-brand design with off-brand photography. Consider making this a standard template feature.

6. **Integration with brand overlay**: Rules for logo placement, text overlay, and colour treatments on photography are the bridge between the photography system and the rest of the visual identity. These rules should live in the photography section (where the photographer/art director will reference them), not solely in the logo section.

### Priority tiers for the template

| Tier | Component | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **Must have** | Philosophy, 3-4 categories, Do/Don't list, Stock rules, Logo-on-photo rules | Present in all top-scoring documents; absence is the primary photography quality gap |
| **Should have** | Colour direction, Casting/diversity, Technical standards | Present in ~60% of top-tier documents; significantly improves consistency |
| **Nice to have** | Photo colour palette, AI/generative imagery rules, Video integration, Retouching limits | Emerging best practice; future-proofs the template |
