## Logo Guidelines

### Wordmark
- Custom wordmark based on **Tachyon typeface**, consisting of four capital letters with equal width
- The shape of the letters is meant to convey the "digital part of dentistry, progress, and stable foundation"
- Top corners of "F" and "A" are designed to highlight "ON" within the brand name — a deliberate embedded message
- Letter spacing defined as half of the font's stem width

### Tagline Position
- Two compositions supported:
  - **Primary**: Single-line tagline beneath wordmark (e.g., "DESIGNED FOR DENTISTS")
  - **Alternative**: Multi-line tagline stacked to the right (e.g., "DESIGNED / FOR DENTISTS / WITH PASSION")

### Clear Space / Safety Area
- Clear space is based on the width of the letter "O" (referred to as X and Y dimensions)
- Three tiers defined:
  - **Minimum safety area** for single use of logo
  - **Optimal safety area** for single use of logo
  - **Minimum safety area** for use in combination with partner/distributor logos
- Proportional system uses fractions: full Y, 1/2 Y, 1/4 Y vertically; 1/2 X horizontally

### Monogram
- A simplified monogram version exists for space-constrained contexts or "more subtle representation" of the brand

### Colour Options
- **Light backgrounds**: Standard colour scheme (turquoise + dark elements)
- **Monochrome printing**: All logo elements in black on white
- **Dark backgrounds**: White or light grey wordmark only; other colour variations explicitly forbidden in negative colour space

### Minimum Size
Not explicitly specified with a pixel/mm value, but the safety area rules imply careful scaling is expected.

## Typography

### Primary Typeface
- **Proxima Nova** (2005, by Mark Simonson Studios)
- Complete reworking of Proxima Sans (1994), expanded to 42 full-featured OpenType fonts
- Described as "a hybrid combining humanistic proportions with a somewhat geometric appearance" — straddling Futura and Akzidenz Grotesk
- Used for: logo type setting, headlines, paragraphs, marketing, and all communications

### Recommended Weights
- **Light** — Headlines
- **Regular** — Body copy, subheadlines, captions
- **ExtraBold** — Sequencer/Title, headings in presentations
- **Bold** — Caption headings (all caps)

### Typographic Hierarchy (6 levels defined)
| Level | Weight | Size | Line-height | Notes |

| Sequencer / Title | ExtraBold | 300% | 1.2 | Largest display level |
| Headline | Light | 200% | 1.2 | |

| Copy | Regular | 100% (base) | 1.5 | |
| Caption Heading | Bold | 80% | 1.5 | All caps |
| Caption Copy | Regular | 80% | 1.5 | |

### System Font Substitutes

## Supporting Graphics

### Light Patterns (Core Graphic Device)
The most distinctive element of the FONA visual identity. "Light Patterns" are mathematically constructed curve systems:
- Built within a grid system using proportions derived from the **golden ratio**
- Curves evolve from one form to another in equidistant steps (e.g., 12, 14, 16, or 20 steps)
- Multiple pattern variants documented across pages 16-26 with precise parameters:

| Pattern | Rotation | Line Colour | Opacity | Crop | Steps |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | 0 deg | Turquoise 50% | 100% | 100% | 16 |
| 1.2 | -45 deg | Turquoise 50% | 100% | 200% | 16 |
| 2.1 | 0 deg | White 100% | 100% | 100% | 14 |
| 2.2 | 45 deg | Turquoise 50% | 100% | 150% | 14 |
| 3.1 | 45 deg | Turquoise 75% 50% 25% | 100% | 100% | 14 |
| 3.2 | 135 deg | Turquoise 75% 50% 25% | 100% | 100% | 14 |
| 4.1 | 45 deg | White 100% | 100% | 100% | 20 |
| 4.2 | -90 deg | White 100% | 100% | 100% | 20 |

- Patterns can be rotated at 0, 45, 90, and 135 degrees
- Combined with colour and gradients, these "define the most essential part of the new FONA visual identity"

## Outliers / Notable Approaches

1. **Light Patterns as a mathematical system**: The golden ratio-based parametric curve system is unusually sophisticated for a dental industry brand. Each pattern is defined by 5 parameters (rotation, line colour, opacity, crop, steps), creating a generative design system rather than static assets. This is more akin to a tech or architectural brand's approach.

2. **Embedded wordmark message**: The deliberate highlighting of "ON" within "FONA" (via the top corners of F and A) is a clever brand detail that adds a secondary meaning layer.

3. **Oracal 641 vinyl specifications**: Including vinyl wrap colour codes (Oracal 641) alongside standard Pantone/RAL values is unusual and shows foresight for environmental/signage applications — relevant for dental trade shows and clinic signage.

4. **Product naming system with vertical bar**: The use of a vertical bar as a product identification mark (page 48) suggests a modular sub-brand architecture, though this is not fully elaborated.

5. **No photography or tone-of-voice guidelines**: For a 66-page brand manual, the complete absence of photography direction and verbal identity guidelines is a notable gap, especially for a healthcare brand where trust-building imagery and messaging are critical.

6. **Comprehensive presentation templates**: The level of detail in the PowerPoint/presentation section (8 pages, covering lists, photo overlays, section headers, pagination) is above average for brand guidelines.
