## Brand Strategy

- **Brand architecture**: DSTRCT.BERLIN is the master brand with three sub-brands: DSTRCT.market, DSTRCT.base, DSTRCT.rise
- **Naming convention**: "DSTRCT." prefix (vowel-stripped "DISTRICT") + dot separator + location/function suffix
- **Sub-brand structure**: Each sub-brand has its own dedicated section (market p.8, base p.11, rise p.14) and distinct color identity
- **Owner**: HB Reavis (copyright 2023)
- **Document**: 36 pages, landscape 1920x1080 format (presentation-style), created in InDesign 18.1
- **Nature**: Identity Update / Concept -- suggests this is an evolution of an existing brand, not a net-new creation

## Logo

- **Wordmark**: "DSTRCT.BERLIN" -- all caps, bold sans-serif, dot separator between DSTRCT and BERLIN
- **Color treatment**: "DSTRCT" portion rendered in brand orange; "BERLIN" in white (on dark) or dark/black (on light)
- **Dual-tone logotype**: The split color within the wordmark is a core identity feature (not just a color variant)
- **Brand color backgrounds**: Logo shown on 4 backgrounds -- orange, warm beige/sand, aqua/mint, deep indigo/navy blue
- **On colored backgrounds**: Entire wordmark appears in white
- **Co-branding**: DSTRCT.BERLIN | hbreavis -- separated by vertical bar, on beige/sand background; HB Reavis logo uses a lighter-weight mixed-case treatment ("hb" light grey, "reavis" dark grey)

## Typography

- **Primary typeface**: Bold, geometric sans-serif (appears custom or heavily modified -- wide letterforms, consistent stroke width)
- **Logo typography**: "DSTRCT" always all-caps bold; sub-brand suffix uses mixed case with lowercase for the function name (e.g., ".base", ".market", ".rise")
- **Case convention**: Master brand = ALL CAPS ("DSTRCT.BERLIN"); sub-brands = CAPS prefix + lowercase suffix ("DSTRCT.base", "DSTRCT.market", "DSTRCT.rise")
- **Weight contrast**: Bold weight used for sub-brand suffixes to differentiate them from the lighter "BERLIN" suffix on the master brand
- **No body text visible** in sampled pages -- guidelines appear to be primarily logo/identity focused rather than full typographic system

## Color Palette

- **Primary orange**: Vibrant, warm orange (approx. #E8611A or similar) -- the dominant brand color, used for "DSTRCT" text and backgrounds
- **Black**: Deep black used as primary background on cover and master brand section
- **Warm beige/sand**: Neutral, earthy warm tone -- used as secondary background and co-branding backdrop
- **Aqua/mint**: Bright turquoise-mint (associated with DSTRCT.rise sub-brand based on contents page)
- **Deep indigo/navy**: Rich dark blue-purple
- **Burgundy/wine red**: Deep red (associated with DSTRCT.market sub-brand per contents page)
- **Teal/dark green**: Deeper teal-green (associated with DSTRCT.base sub-brand per contents page)
- **White**: Used for text on dark/colored backgrounds
- **Sub-brand color coding**: Each sub-brand gets a unique color -- market=burgundy, base=teal, rise=mint/aqua

## Graphic Elements

- **Sub-brand color grid**: Consistent 2x2 grid layout showing each sub-brand logo on its own color + alternate brand colors (teal, orange, beige, black for DSTRCT.base)
- **Sub-brand + master lockup**: "DSTRCT.BERLIN | DSTRCT.base" horizontal lockup separated by vertical bar, on beige background -- mirrors the HB Reavis co-branding pattern
- **Modular color block system**: All sub-brands use the same 4-up grid layout template, suggesting a systematic design approach across the family
- **Color cross-pollination**: Sub-brands borrow colors from other sub-brands for alternate backgrounds (e.g., DSTRCT.base shown on burgundy and orange backgrounds that belong to other sub-brands)

## Applications

- **Claim on colored backgrounds**: "Work. Eat. Meet." shown in oversized type on deep indigo and aqua/mint backgrounds (p.19) -- tone-on-tone treatment (lighter tint of background color used for text)
- **Claim on white**: Large-scale orange claim text on white background (p.18) with explanatory body copy below
- Remaining application pages (pp. 20-36) not sampled -- likely contain signage, environmental, digital mockups

## Tone of Voice

- **Brand claim**: "Work. Eat. Meet." -- three single-word sentences, each ending with a period
- **Claim rationale**: The triad explains the three functions of the ensemble: Work = office areas, Eat = restaurants/cafes/grocery stores, Meet = indoor and outdoor meeting areas
- **Verbal style**: Terse, imperative, action-oriented -- stripped-down verbs mirroring the vowel-stripped brand name (DSTRCT)
- **Punctuation as design**: Period after each word is a deliberate stylistic device, echoing the dot separator in the brand name

## Outliers / Unique Elements

- **Vowel-stripping naming**: "DSTRCT" removes all vowels from "DISTRICT" -- distinctive linguistic device carried across the entire brand family
- **Dot as structural separator**: The period/dot in "DSTRCT.BERLIN" is both punctuation and a visual identity element, consistently used across all sub-brands
- **Tone-on-tone claim treatment**: The "Work. Eat. Meet." claim rendered in a lighter/darker shade of the background color (not contrasting color) creates a subtle, textural effect
- **Mixed-use real estate as lifestyle brand**: The guidelines position what is essentially a commercial development with the visual confidence and editorial tone of a consumer lifestyle brand
- **No hex/RGB/CMYK values observed**: Color specifications not found in sampled pages -- may exist in unsampled pages or a separate document
