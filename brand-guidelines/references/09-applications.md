# Section 7 — Applications

Place Applications after the visual-system sections. Use it to show how logo, color, typography, graphics, and photography work together in real contexts.

## How many applications — by tier

| Tier | Application count | Examples |
|------|------------------:|----------|
| Compact | 5 | Business card, social post, presentation slide, email signature, one large-format |
| Standard | 8–12 | Above + letterhead, report cover, brochure, signage, swag |

Applications prove that the system works. Choose examples that match the brand's actual channels and expected production needs.

## Use REAL content, not lorem ipsum

Use brand-specific content such as:

- A resort brand — actual menu prices, real staff names, specific vehicle models
- A fitness platform — real athlete profiles and real activity data, never lorem
- A global bank — real account types and real city names in every mockup

Lorem ipsum reads as a placeholder document. Real content reads as a product. If the user can't supply content, MAKE realistic placeholder content (real-sounding names, plausible prices, believable copy) — not "Lorem ipsum dolor sit."

## Application — anatomy of a good one

For each application, include:

1. **The mockup** — high-fidelity, realistic, full-bleed where appropriate
2. **Specifications** — paper stock / format / dimensions / printing method
3. **Asset references** — link to the InDesign / Figma / template file
4. **Variant guidance** — when there are multiple appropriate versions
5. **Production notes** — printer notes, cutting marks, bleeds where relevant

### Example: Business card

```
## Business Card

Format:        85 × 55 mm (standard EU) or 88.9 × 50.8 mm (US)
Stock:         350 gsm uncoated; soft-touch lamination optional
Print:         CMYK + 1 spot color (PANTONE 2727 C); recommended litho or letterpress
Colorways:     Approved variants — front A, B; back A, B, C
Bleed:         3 mm all sides
Safe area:     5 mm from edge

Layout:
  Front:       Logo top-left; name and role in Söhne Halbfett 11pt; contact in 9pt
  Back:        Mark only; or single brand statement at 24pt

Files:         /Brand/Templates/Stationery/business-card.indd
               /Brand/Templates/Stationery/business-card.afpub
```

### Example: Social media post (1080×1080)

```
## Instagram / LinkedIn — square post (1080 × 1080 px)

Safe area:     80px from each edge (Instagram crops in feed)
Type:          H1 ≤ 4 lines at 72px; subhead ≤ 3 lines at 36px
Logo:          Bottom-right corner; 80px from edges; mark-only at 64px height
Color rule:    One brand color + one neutral; never mix two brand colors
Photo:         Apply LUT v2.1; never re-grade in-app

Templates:     /Brand/Templates/Social/IG-post.psd, .fig
```

## Templates as deliverables

Point to actual templates whenever possible and include them in the asset bundle:

- **InDesign** for stationery and editorial
- **Figma libraries** for digital and presentation
- **PowerPoint / Keynote** for non-designers
- **Canva** for social if the brand uses it
- **Office (Word / Excel / Outlook)** for internal docs

The PDF guide explains the rules; the templates enforce them. A brand without templates relies on every designer reading the guide carefully — fragile.

## Don'ts

- **Generic stock-mockup overlays** — corner-curled business cards on wood textures. Replace with brand-realistic mockups.
- **Single mockup per application type** — show 2–3 variants where the system allows
- **Application without specifications** — the mockup is half the work; specs are the other half
- **Lorem ipsum** — every line of placeholder text degrades the document
- **Mixing tier-mismatched apps** — don't show a Compact-tier business card next to a Comprehensive-tier multi-spread brochure

## Anti-patterns

- **Applications-only document.** Strong logo rules, applications, nothing else. Usable but shallow — it tells people where the mark goes without telling them what the brand means.
- **Aspirational mockups.** Showing applications the brand will never produce (Olympic-stadium-billboard for a SaaS startup). Embarrassing in audit.
- **No source files.** Mockups without templates create a "looks great in PDF, never used in reality" gap.
