# Writing the Document Itself

The guidelines document is also a brand artifact. Its writing should deliberately use either the brand's own voice or a neutral reference register based on audience and purpose.

This reference governs **how the document itself sounds**, not the brand it documents.

## Three writing modes

| Mode | Use when |
|------|----------|
| Hybrid (narrative + spec) | The reader needs both strategic context and production rules |
| Instructional / Technical | The document serves compliance, partners, or implementation teams |
| Inspirational / Narrative | The document is principles-led and detailed specifications live elsewhere |

**Default to hybrid.** Open sections with narrative context explaining purpose, then transition to precise specifications. Answer both "why" and "how."

Pure instructional writing works for partner, compliance, or engineering-grade documents. Pure inspirational writing works only for principles-led books whose detailed specifications live elsewhere.

**Default to hybrid unless the brief specifies otherwise.**

## Voice — neutral or brand?

| Mode | Use when |
|------|----------|
| Neutral / consultant register | Compliance, partner, regulated, multi-stakeholder, or large-enterprise use |
| Brand voice (the doc sounds like the brand) | Default for hybrid documents and brand-facing audiences |
| Minimal text | Pure specification or visual reference |

**Defaults**:
- Internal team / agency-led / consumer brands → **brand voice**
- B2B / enterprise / regulated industries → **neutral but warm**
- Government / civic / safety-critical → **neutral and precise**

## Tactics for writing in brand voice

If voice = brand voice, every paragraph should pass this test: "Could this sentence appear in our marketing copy?"

Concrete moves:

1. **Use the brand's own opening greeting.** A greeting in the founders' language; a pronunciation joke about the brand name; a cheerful "Yay, it's Monday!" Set the tone in the first line of the foreword.
2. **First-person plural ("we", "our"), not third-person ("the brand", "[BrandName]").** "We don't use Helvetica" reads as a brand decision; "[BrandName] does not use Helvetica" reads as a contractor's note.
3. **Match sentence length to brand cadence.** Punchy brand → short sentences. Premium / luxury → measured cadence with longer phrases. Technical → precise but not robotic.
4. **Embed brand vocabulary.** If the brand says "athletes, never users," the doc itself uses "athletes" when referring to the people the brand serves.
5. **Use brand-style headings.** If the brand uses sentence-case for everything, headings are sentence-case here too.
6. **Demonstrate the voice in examples.** A TOV section that says "we use direct, irreverent headlines" should be written in direct, irreverent prose.

## Phrasing rules

### Pair "Do" with "Don't"

Lead with the affirmative treatment, then show the negative treatment for contrast. Never publish a section that contains only "don'ts."

### Soft vs hard rules

The strictness of language should scale with the risk of the element:

| Element | Recommended phrasing |
|---------|---------------------|
| Logo, color values, legal | Always / Never / Must / Under no circumstances |
| Typography hierarchy, application templates | Use / Avoid / Standard is / Default to |
| Photography style, voice | Prefer / Lean toward / In most cases / Generally |

Examples: "Under no circumstances is the symbol to be redrawn." "Contractions preferred."
A modern design lab: "Principles rather than hard rules."

Match the strictness to the stakes.

### "Things to Avoid" reframe

When appropriate for the brand voice, replace "Don't" with softer alternatives:

- "Things to Avoid"
- "Avoidances" (e.g. "No mascot puns; no 'shoot/shot' language")
- "Sensitivity guidance" — framed as cultural awareness rather than prohibition

This preserves the practical value of negative examples while sounding less authoritarian. Use this for brands whose voice is collaborative or educational.

## Section-opening templates

Each canonical section should open with 1–2 paragraphs of narrative context. Use these prompts to draft:

### Brand Strategy
> "Before we get to colors and typefaces, here's why [brand] exists and who we're for. The rest of this document follows from these decisions."

### Logo
> "Our logo is the most concentrated expression of who we are. These rules protect that concentration. Following them keeps the mark powerful at any scale, in any context."

### Typography
> "Type carries most of what people read about us. Get the typography right and the brand reads as serious; get it wrong and nothing else holds up."

### Color
> "Colors carry feeling before words do. Our palette is the emotional infrastructure of the brand. These specifications make sure the feeling lands the same on screen, in print, and across every supplier."

### Graphics
> "[Named device] is more than decoration — it's a signature. Wherever it appears, the brand is unmistakable. These rules keep the device recognizable and reproducible."

### Photography
> "Photography is where the brand becomes a place, a person, a moment. The photography we commission and curate is held to one standard: it has to feel like us."

### Applications
> "All the rules in this document only matter when they leave the document. This section shows the brand in the wild — on screens, on paper, on walls, in hands."

### Tone of Voice
> "Voice is what we sound like when no one's watching. Tone is how that voice flexes by context. This section makes the difference legible to anyone writing on our behalf."

Adapt these to brand cadence — they're scaffolding, not template.

## Format conventions for the document

- **Page numbering**: required for documents 20+ pages
- **Section headers**: clear, hierarchical, ideally numbered (1.1, 1.2)
- **TOC**: required for documents 30+ pages
- **Caption sizing**: distinct from body, smaller, often in muted color
- **Asset references**: every spec links to its file/repo
- **Quotation / pull quotes**: for impactful brand statements
- **Whitespace**: use generous whitespace; avoid cramped layouts

## File / publishing

- **Target file size**: under 50 MB. Documents at 200–300 MB are an anti-pattern — a 30-page deck has no business weighing 300 MB, and nobody opens it twice.
- **PDF**: optimized for screen (downsampled images at 150 DPI for screen, 300 DPI for print)
- **Versioning**: every shipped document includes a version number on cover and footer
- **Cover**: brand name, document type ("Brand Guidelines"), version, publication date
- **Back cover**: contact, asset locations, governance

## Anti-patterns

- **Corporate filler.** "This document serves as a comprehensive resource for all stakeholders engaged with the brand identity." Cut every sentence like this.
- **Generic openings.** "In today's competitive landscape..." — never.
- **Inconsistent voice across sections.** If chapters were written by different people, audit and unify.
- **Boilerplate from a previous brand's guidelines.** Recognize copy that didn't get tailored.
- **Excessive screenshots of the doc itself.** A guidelines document doesn't need to show off — show the brand, not the document's design.
