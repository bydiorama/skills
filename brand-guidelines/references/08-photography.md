# Section 6 — Photography

Position 6 (70% consensus). Present in 77.2% of the corpus.

**The single strongest quality differentiator in the entire corpus.** Documents WITH dedicated photography sections average 4.0/5; without, 3.0/5. Among 5/5 documents, 97% include photography; among 2/5 documents, only 44% — a +53 percentage-point delta, the largest gap of any section.

Including photography direction signals a mature, investment-grade brand system.

## When to include — and when to placeholder

Include the section if any of these are true:
- The brand has any existing photography library
- The brand operates in a category where photography is core (hospitality, real estate, F&B, fashion, lifestyle, retail)
- The brand has budget to commission shoots in the next 12 months
- The brand uses stock photography (still needs direction)

Placeholder if:
- The brand is purely illustration-led (Byteminds D027 — uses 3D rendering instead)
- The brand is digital-only with no human/object subjects (some SaaS, B2B infrastructure)
- The brand is pre-product, pre-revenue, with no shoots planned

A placeholder section is still better than omission.

## The named-categories approach

Top-scoring documents define 3–6 named photo categories, each with a clear role. Examples:

### Adobe (E019, 5/5) — three named tiers

| Category | Role | Description |
|----------|------|-------------|
| Atmospheric | Hero / brand-mood | Wide, environmental, light-led, no faces |
| Depersonalized | Product / context | Hands at work, objects in use, no identifying faces |
| Personal | Story / human | Identifiable subjects, full face, named or quoted |

### Nike Empower (E146, 5/5) — empowered creative network

25% creator freedom / 75% brand cohesion model. Categories include hero campaign, social-content, athlete-led, community-led.

### NOVEBA (D033, 4.5/5) — property-specific

Categorized: building exteriors, interiors, environmental context, lifestyle context, with do's and don'ts per category.

### Strava (E090, 5/5) — "imperfectly beautiful"

Athlete-led, never-staged, real-conditions photography principle. Anti-glossy.

**Default starter set for most brands:**
1. **Hero / Brand** — emotional, atmospheric, used at largest scale
2. **People / Story** — humans, often customers or staff
3. **Product / Object** — what the brand makes or sells
4. **Environment / Context** — where the brand operates
5. **Lifestyle / Use** — the brand in real situations
6. **Detail / Texture** — close-ups, supporting imagery

## Photography philosophy

Open the section with a short philosophy statement (3–6 sentences) in the brand's voice. Examples:

- **Strava**: "Imperfectly beautiful. Real athletes, real conditions, real moments. Never staged, never airbrushed."
- **Vratna (D017)**: "Photography for Vratna captures the slow rhythm of mountain time. Long lenses, soft light, no rush. People are present but not posed."
- **Adobe**: "Atmospheric, depersonalized, or personal — never decorative."

The philosophy is the rule everything else hangs on.

## Mood / tonal direction

Specify with both adjectives AND visual references:

```
Mood:        Honest, weathered, sun-touched, never glossy
Light:       Natural daylight; golden hour preferred; soft shadows ok
Color:       Warm whites, blue-toned shadows, never highly saturated
Contrast:    Medium; never crushed blacks
Texture:     Visible film grain or fine digital noise; never smooth
```

Pair these specs with a 6–12 image mood board showing approved direction.

## Composition rules

| Topic | Default rule |
|-------|-------------|
| Framing | Rule of thirds; allow off-center for editorial feel |
| Negative space | Ample; allows for headlines and logo placement |
| Subject placement | Eye-line in upper third for portraits |
| Depth | Foreground / midground / background layered |
| Angle | Eye-level for human subjects; never overhead unless tabletop |
| Crop | Never crop limbs at joints; allow bleed for energy shots |

Document any brand-specific rules:
- "Never crop the building below the second floor" (real estate)
- "Always show hands and food in the same frame" (F&B)
- "Subjects always look toward the headline, never away" (campaign work)

## Color treatment / grading

If photography needs to feel cohesive across photographers, document the grade:

- **Color profile**: sRGB for web, Adobe RGB for print, ProPhoto for archival
- **White balance**: 5200K daylight default; 3200K tungsten allowed for interior
- **Saturation**: −5 to +5 LUT range; never beyond
- **Highlights / Shadows**: specific values
- **LUT file**: provided in the asset bundle

Vratna and NOVEBA both ship LUT recommendations. This is a competitive edge.

## Subject and casting

For brands featuring people:

- **Diversity** — explicit casting principles (age, ethnicity, body type, gender expression, ability)
- **Authenticity** — real customers / staff vs hired talent; mix or one-only?
- **Wardrobe** — restrictions (no logos other than the brand's; no extreme patterns)
- **Expression** — natural, candid, vs posed; range and permitted intensities
- **Setting** — own venues vs studio vs found locations

Strava's "Athletes, never users" language extends to casting: real athletes only, no models posing as athletes.

## Stock photography — when allowed

Many brands rely on stock. Document the approval process:

- **Approved sources** — Stocksy, Unsplash+, Adobe Stock, Getty, custom commissioned
- **Forbidden tells** — generic handshakes, white-background isolated objects, "diverse team in office" cliché shots, exaggerated emotions
- **Approval workflow** — who signs off; turnaround time
- **License tracking** — where licenses are stored

Wave (E064) takes the unusual position of including Midjourney prompts as official asset creation methodology — early signal of AI-generated imagery normalizing for stock-replacement.

## Don'ts — show, don't tell

Show 3–6 anti-examples with red ✗ overlay or "DO NOT" labeling. Common categories:

- **Staged corporate stock** — generic conference rooms, fake handshakes
- **Heavy filters / Instagram presets** — drastically saturated, vignetted, dehazed
- **Inconsistent grading** — half the photos warm, half cool
- **Over-airbrushing** — skin smoothing, eye whitening, body reshaping
- **Bad cropping** — heads cut, limbs at joints, awkward negative space
- **Watermarks visible** — un-licensed stock leaking through

## Commissioning process

For brands that will shoot regularly:

```
1. Brief written by [marketing/brand lead]
2. Photographer selected from approved list (or RFP)
3. Pre-production: shot list, location, casting, props approved by [brand director]
4. On-set brand representative recommended for first shoot
5. Selects + grade reviewed against this guide
6. Final assets uploaded to [DAM] with metadata + tags
```

## Anti-patterns

- **Mood board only.** A pinboard of nice photos is not direction.
- **Photography "vibe" without specs.** "Editorial" means nothing without composition rules.
- **No don'ts.** The most common failure mode for photo sections.
- **No real-world examples.** Show images that exist, not impossible compositions.
- **Different rules per channel** without a unifying principle.

## When the brand has no photography assets

Write a "photography intent" page:

```
## Photography — direction (assets in development)

Photography for [brand] will follow these principles when commissioned:

- [Mood adjective], [mood adjective], never [opposite]
- [Light type] preferred; [contrast level] grading
- [Subject focus]
- [Three reference photographers or styles]

A full photography library will be commissioned in [Q3 2026]. Until then,
defer to typography and color as the primary brand expression in any
photography-required moment.
```
