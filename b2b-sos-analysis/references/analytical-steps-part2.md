# Analytical Steps — Part 2 (Steps 9–16)

These are the advanced analysis and strategic translation steps. Execute them after completing all steps in Part 1.

---

## STEP 9 — Decision criteria & Reasons to Believe

Analyse how buyers choose vendors or solutions and what credibility evidence appears in search.

**Track these criteria:**
- Certifications
- Accuracy / precision
- Compatibility
- Integration
- Price / cost
- Simplicity / ease of use
- Implementation speed
- Scalability
- Support quality
- Trust / brand reputation
- Industry fit

**Questions to answer:**
- Which selection criteria recur most frequently?
- Which are hygiene factors (must-have) and which can be sources of differentiation?
- Which brands are most associated with which criteria?
- Which RTBs (Reasons to Believe) does the client clearly need to be credible?

**Output:** "Decision criteria / RTB map."

---

## STEP 10 — Brand mental association map

Perform search-based mental mapping of brands.

**Analyse:**
- Recurring attributes paired with brands
- Usage contexts
- Industries / verticals
- Use cases
- Comparison pairings (which brands get compared to which)
- Brand-anchored clusters

**Questions to answer:**
- What is each significant brand relatively more associated with?
- Are these associations consistent over time?
- Does the client have clear search associations or is it fragmented?
- Which mental territories are occupied and which are more open?

**Output:** "Brand mental association map."

**Language rule:** Always use cautious phrasing:
- ✅ "relative search association"
- ❌ "proven mental ownership"

---

## STEP 11 — Segment-specific CEP relevance

Identify whether some CEP patterns are tied to specific market types, industries, or use cases.

**Questions to answer:**
- Is the CEP universal or segment-specific?
- Which industries or use-case terms recur?
- Where would it be a mistake to generalise a local pattern to the whole market?
- Which priorities need to be read through a segment filter?

**Output:** "Segment-specific CEP fit note."

**Confidence label:** Always mark findings as either:
- "Confirmed only partially"
- "Working hypothesis"

...unless there is strong data support.

---

## STEP 12 — Regional granularity (if data permits)

When sub-market regional breakdowns are available, analyse:
- Leaders per region
- CEP clusters per region
- Barriers per region
- Value signals per region
- Brand associations per region

**Questions to answer:**
- Do only the brands differ by region, or does the demand logic itself differ?
- Are some CEPs regionally stronger?
- Does the language, the barriers, or the default choice change by region?

**Output:** "Regional demand logic note."

**If data does not support regional analysis**, state this explicitly and move on. Do not speculate.

---

## STEP 13 — Barriers, friction & negative CEPs

Map reasons for concern and friction in the buying process.

Work with four barrier types:
1. **Economic** — price, budget, ROI uncertainty
2. **Implementation** — complexity, migration, integration effort
3. **Organisational** — internal politics, change management, training
4. **Trust / risk** — vendor risk, data security, reliability concerns

**Questions to answer:**
- Which barriers recur most frequently?
- Are they tied to the category as a whole or only to specific solutions?
- At which CEPs do they appear?
- Should they be addressed through messaging, positioning, product, or the sales process?

**Output:** "Barrier map."

---

## STEP 14 — Default choice & category mental standard

Determine who is the default choice and who sets the category norm.

**Questions to answer:**
- Who is the implicit benchmark in the category?
- Who sets the reference language?
- Where does the client compete head-to-head against the default and where can it find its own sub-space?
- Is it smarter to attack the dominant standard frontally or to route around it?

**Output:** "Default choice note."

---

## STEP 15 — NO-GO zones & deliberate exclusion

**This step is mandatory.** For every relevant cluster, explicitly decide where the brand should NOT go.

**Questions to answer:**
- Which spaces are methodologically unclean (noisy, ambiguous data)?
- Which are overcrowded with no realistic entry path?
- Which would unnecessarily dilute the brand?
- Where would the firm be chasing search noise rather than strategic meaning?
- Which clusters are suitable only for monitoring, not activation?

**Output:** "NO-GO register" — a clear list with rationale for each exclusion.

---

## STEP 16 — Strategic translation to downstream modules

Translate all findings into the client's strategic language.

**Produce clear inputs for:**
- Value proposition
- Positioning
- Brand platform
- Sales messaging
- Content and communication

**Questions to answer:**
- In which situations should the brand build long-term mental presence?
- What value should it promise in those situations?
- What RTBs must support that promise?
- What should the brand deliberately avoid?
- What are the 3–5 most important strategic implications for downstream work?

**Output:** "Strategic translation note."

---

## Closing sections — mandatory

After all 17 steps are complete, append these four sections at the very end:

### 1. "Most probable picture of the market according to SoS"
A brief synthesis of market reality as the data paints it.

### 2. "Strongest strategic opportunities"
Maximum 5 points. Each must reference the data that supports it.

### 3. "What to avoid"
Maximum 5 points. Each must explain why avoidance is warranted.

### 4. "What needs further validation"
Things SoS suggests but does not prove — items that need CRM data, qualitative research, sales feedback, or other sources to confirm.
