# Diorama Brand Strategy Prompts

## Skill Creation
Create a comprehensive Claude Code skill to create a brand strategy based on input from client interviews and workshops, as well as additional online research focused on the client's category, long-term and short-term trends affecting their category, competitors, and potential opportunities from both brand and business perspectives. Additional resources include the final brand strategies from previous projects.

## Conduct Research
### Payment Processing and Merchant Services
Conduct comprehensive category research for the U.S. credit card processing and merchant service provider market with the following objectives:

- Identify the main categories, subcategories, and category entry points (the situations or triggers that prompt businesses to seek merchant services or switch providers) within the credit card processing and merchant services space in the U.S.
- Map the competitive landscape, including leading national and regional brands, emerging players, product/service models, and technology trends.
- Analyze category norms regarding pricing and fee structures, customer support models, transparency, educational resources, and brand positioning.
- Highlight areas of opportunity or differentiation for IMS—where category pain points, dissatisfaction, or gaps exist that IMS can address.
- Summarize typical customer expectations, frustrations, and key entry points that drive merchant decision-making in this category, and compare them with IMS’s current brand promise and messaging.
- Review regulatory, compliance, and industry trends (PCI, surcharging, interchange, etc.) shaping the space.
- Provide visual and narrative examples of category positioning, tone, and frequently used language in top competitor marketing.

Deliver actionable insights and recommendations that IMS can leverage for strategic brand, messaging, and positioning decisions.

### Local Real Estate in the Spiš Region of Slovakia
Analyze the real estate market in the Spiš region of Slovakia (specifically Spišská Nová Ves, Levoča, and Spišské Podhradie) to identify short-term trends, such as the demand for 'small units' that sell quickly, and long-term trends, including the increasing frequency with which 25–45-year-olds now trade property compared to previous generations. Identify Category Entry Points (CEPs) for sellers and buyers, focusing on investment properties for short-term rental near UNESCO sites and young people looking to divest or upgrade from their first properties. Finally, highlight growth opportunities for a firm positioned as a professional, collective 'IKEA-style' brand(accessible but highly standardized) to differentiate itself from local 'one-man show' competitors through transparency in commissions, mortgage services, and professional presentation.

Key Areas the Research Should Cover:
* Target Demographics: Focus on the 25–45 age bracket, as they are increasingly willing to pay for full-service property management to save time.
* Investment Climate: Investigate the 'UNESCO effect' in Spišské Podhradie, where tourist traffic and the potential for short-term rental yields (e.g., via AirBnB or Booking.com) are major purchase motives.
* Competitive Landscape: Look for gaps left by fragmented local agencies that lack a cohesive visual identity, transparent fee structures, or professional dress codes.
* Service Diversification: Explore the feasibility of adding "complementary" services such as administrative support for building permits, geodetic surveys, and professional home staging, which align with the vision of becoming a regional market leader.
